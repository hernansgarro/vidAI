# IDEA-026 — Chemical scents, olfaction, and pheromone traces

> **State:** Exploring. First expanded discussion: 2026-11-03. Personal contributor notes only. This is neither an approved specification nor authorization for implementation, experiments, an upstream issue/PR, release, or deployment.
>
> [Personal idea card](IDEA_LAB.md#idea-026-chemical-scents-olfaction-and-pheromone-traces) · [Earlier related mention: upstream #118](https://github.com/smeagol44/vidAI/issues/118) · [Shared roadmap #57](https://github.com/smeagol44/vidAI/issues/57)

## Motivation and Hernan's initial proposal

Create an evolvable chemical sensory and signaling system, independent of optical RGB vision. **Current proposed direct emitters:** (1) inorganic/organic nutrients, (2) eggs, (3) finite pheromone/perfume objects, and **(4) living creatures emitting their own slightly stronger body-scent pulses directly**. Creatures **also** involuntarily deposit weak, short-lived pheromone objects with their inherited scent signature; optional brain-controlled glands could produce further pheromones. This explicitly supersedes the prior 'creatures never directly emit scent' decision. Each source periodically releases discrete scent circles that follow one universal expansion-and-decay rule. Organisms with noses receive the local floral/herbal/mineral concentrations into their brains; behavior, tracking, avoidance and communication must emerge without scripted goals.

### Proposed three-channel scent and observer colors

Hernan initially suggested **floral (R), herbal (G), mineral (B)**. His initial text repeated G for the third component, apparently a channel-letter typo. This is a *provisional analogy*, not a real-world chemical taxonomy or a finalized genetic representation.

| Emitter | Initial illustrative scent | Optional human-visible halo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inorganic nutrient | **Only G**, now conceptually associated with minerals/inorganic matter | Green |
| Organic nutrient | **Only R**, conceptually associated with blood/meat/organic remains | Red |
| Creature body and passive pheromones | **B-dominant inherited signature**; minor, variable R and G contributions may coexist | Blue-dominant mixture |
| Brain-controlled gland pheromone | Inherited signature with more compositional freedom, initially possibly B-dominant | Corresponding color mix |

**Naming refinement (2026-11-03):** Keep evocative metaphorical names, never anonymous A/B/C axes. The user now prefers **R associated with blood-like organic nutrients, G with inorganic/mineral nutrients, and B with living animal bodies**. The earlier floral/herbal/mineral labels are historical, not settled; **bloodlike/sanguine (R), mineral (G), and musky (B)** is a proposed mnemonic triplet, **not yet approved**. "Musky" (almizclado) refers to animalic/skin-associated fragrance notes, though modern synthetic musks can also smell clean or powdery. "Sweet" was considered for organic nutrients but rejected as potentially confusable with a future taste sense. Strongly prefer an R-only signature for organic nutrients, G-only for inorganic nutrients, and a genetically variable B-dominant signature for creatures. Gland signatures may vary more freely, initially perhaps still B-dominant. Example hexadecimal signatures `A00000`, `008000`, `1020C0` and `8040B0` are demonstrations only, not authorized genetics/defaults. Scent channels are not creature-visible optical pigments.

### Proposed hexadecimal smell/display notation (2026-11-03)

- **RRGGBBTT:** RR/GG/BB are a pulse's **fixed inherited/source fragrance signature**; the current source associations are bloodlike organic matter (R), mineral/inorganic matter (G), and musky/animal bodies (B), subject to final mnemonic naming. Each signature byte ranges **00–FF**. **A pulse never chemically changes its RRGGBB signature as it ages.** All three contributions fade together, retaining their proportions.
- **One scalar intensity:** the pulse has a separate scalar intensity, declining over time/expansion, which modulates the contribution of its fixed signature. The user explicitly wants **1:1 normalized identity between pulse intensity and the human-visible alpha/opacity byte TT**, without a separate visually tuned fade curve. In a possible byte model `TT = intensityByte`, with **00 = no intensity/fully transparent** and **FF = maximum intensity/fully opaque**. This follows CSS alpha convention (`#RRGGBBAA`); the user called it transparency, but opacity/alpha is the unambiguous interpretation.
- **Illustrative receiver calculation, still subject to scaling decisions:** for a nose inside a uniformly strong pulse, `receivedComponent = signatureComponent * TT / 255`, followed by channel-wise addition across all intersecting pulses and saturation at the receiver's FF bound. For instance signature `A00000` (red component 160 decimal), TT `80` (128 decimal), contributes approximately 80 red units at that nose. This is a *candidate mapping*, not agreed source/brain units. If an optional radial gradient is introduced, specify its additional spatial multiplier consistently in both sensory and observer presentation; one uniform TT per whole circle alone cannot depict a different local opacity at every radius.
- A fixed `RRGGBB` and a changing `TT` therefore give the UI a **constant hue/composition but diminishing opacity**, while the nose sees proportionally weaker inputs. TT is **not a fourth olfactory channel** or sensory input. An overlay made by standard alpha compositing of multiple colored circles is not mathematically identical to additive/saturating sensor summation; decide later if a combined observer field needs exact diagnostic parity.
- Each component is a byte, so hexadecimal TT spans **00–FF**, not decimal 00–99. The saturation limit FF applies to each receiver channel *after* sums, not to a total brightness across all three components. Set actual input scaling/rounding and saturation handling before implementation.

### Emission, spatial range and decay

- Proposed spectator visualization: a circular halo centered on the source, optionally shown. **Opacity** represents concentration/intensity and **radius** represents the *effective reach* of a scent rather than a direct measurement of chemical amount. The user's clarified preference is for a scent to **grow in reach and then fade away**, not necessarily shrink.
- A living creature **directly emits inherited body-scent circles**, tentatively somewhat stronger than those emitted by its involuntary passive pheromone objects; it **also deposits passive pheromone objects** at its center of mass or another attachment point to be decided. Both mechanisms use the shared pulse growth/decay model. Direct circles can leave short residual traces after movement, while deposited pheromone emitters can extend the trail.
- **Refined unified model (2026-11-03; supersedes the earlier two-phase plume model):** every emitter periodically releases a discrete **scent pulse**, represented by an independently evolving circle. All released scent pulses follow the same lifecycle regardless of their source: a pulse starts with a source-configured initial intensity, expands over simulation time, loses intensity as it expands, decays additionally beyond dilution, and is removed entirely when its perceptible concentration falls below a defined cutoff. A mathematically exact real-valued zero is not required for removal.
- **Finite pheromone source:** a pheromone droplet/object is proposed to contain a small finite number of **perfume units**, illustratively 1–10 or 1–20 (not settled bounds). At its configured emission cadence it consumes a perfume unit to create a scent pulse. Its remaining perfume units decrease; the emitter disappears when exhausted. Previously released scent pulses continue to disperse and expire independently. The source can configure the initial intensity of its pulses, distinct from its number of remaining units.
- **Persistent sources:** nutrient particles, eggs, **and living creatures** may emit scent pulses directly as long as they exist; nutrients/eggs tentatively pulse less often, and creature body-scent pulses may be somewhat stronger than their involuntary passive pheromone pulses. Creatures **additionally** deposit finite passive pheromone objects. All circles use the same aging law. Conceptually unlimited source perfume units do **not** grant free chemical energy or an exception from conservation. Exact cadence, intensity and source cost remain unresolved.
- **Resulting gradient:** a cadence of pulses creates near-source fresh/strong circles and farther-reaching older/weaker circles; local readings should reflect the concentration at each nose. This does not strictly guarantee a smooth or monotonically decreasing gradient if the pulses are infrequent, emitters move or their circles overlap. Do not hard-code an attraction vector or emitter position.
- **Independent dimensions:** perfume-unit reserve, pulse emission frequency, initial scent intensity, **fixed inherited/source R/G/B fragrance signature**, current pulse radius, local concentration and decay lifetime are conceptually distinct. A single intensity scalar decreases as each pulse ages, shared by all three fixed signature channels and 1:1 with its human-visible alpha. The suggested 1–20 capacity is illustrative only; emitted units, frequency, spatial falloff, radius growth and expiration threshold have not been numerically chosen.
- **Earlier two-phase idea is superseded:** scent release no longer requires a distinct 'core-supported cloud' state followed by a separate 'source-free cloud' diffusion state. The material pheromone source simply produces multiple independent pulses, each with one universal expansion/fading rule.
- **Accelerated fading confirmed as design intent:** scent intensity must fall **faster than the inverse of the expanding circle's area**, to limit CPU load, sensory background and visual clutter. Hernan's non-binding example: area 1 with intensity 10, area 2 with intensity 4 (rather than 5 from geometric dilution), area 4 with intensity 1 (rather than 2.5), then intensity 0 on a subsequent tick and **complete circle deletion**. A simple candidate is an additional decay multiplier below 1 on top of area dilution, but this alone approaches zero asymptotically; an explicit finite-lifetime, zero-clamping, or cutoff rule will be needed. No numerical coefficient or threshold is chosen. Expired pulses must be removed from **authoritative simulation state**, not merely hidden in the renderer. Historical scent persistence and closed-energy accounting still require separate decisions.
- These principles are an evolutionary-simulation design direction, not a measured real-world diffusion model. Whether concentration varies with distance from the core (e.g. stronger near the center), whether field movement follows currents, and exact growth/fade curves are **undecided**.
- Clarify emitted quantity, local concentration, source strength, effective radius, falloff, decay rate, lifetime and behavior when the original source moves or disappears.
- **Overlap rule now agreed at the conceptual level:** the nose independently **sums all local floral, herbal and mineral concentration contributions** from every overlapping pulse. For example, floral 3 from one circle plus floral 5 from another produces floral 8 at that nose. A per-channel saturation bound applies, but ordinary scents are intended to stay far from that bound; its value and nonlinear/linear saturation semantics remain open. Positive-only additive contributions cannot subtract or chemically neutralize an existing scent. Emission/pulse accumulation must not quietly overwhelm the sensors or CPU; rendering layers never determine what organisms smell.
- Water barriers, tank edges, local tubes and potential transfer between connected water regions need explicit behavior. A human hide/show toggle must never disable actual sensing or accelerate/stop scent decay.

### Olfactory organs and brain inputs

- Proposed noses sample three actual local chemical components when inside the field, passing them into the creature's brain through a sensory pathway analogous to vision, **not** through existing optical RGB or secret source identifiers.
- Open: genetic presence, number and position of noses; relative receptor sensitivity; normalization, saturation, refresh cadence, possible noise and construction/maintenance costs.
- A single local sample gives **concentration, not source direction**. Gradient following should only be possible through different sampling positions, changes over time and neural memory, or another explicitly modeled mechanism.
- Organism odors are genetically determined and inheritable/mutable. Shared scent similarity is not an automatic parent/offspring identifier.

### Passive odor trails versus active glands

**Involuntary traces (revised):** The creature **does directly produce its own body-scent pulses**, and **in addition** it periodically deposits small, short-lived **passive pheromone objects** carrying its inherited scent signature. Each passive object has a small finite perfume-unit supply and releases that many weak scent circles at its own frequency, after which the object disappears while any already-emitted circles continue fading. Hernan suggested **one deposited pheromone per second** and **three weak perfume units per object** solely as illustrative numbers, not set parameters. The passive emissions are inevitable as a species/creature scent mark, independently of optional brain-command glands. Emitter frequency, total marker/pulse population limits, stationary deposition, drift, lifespan and passive metabolic cost remain open. Markers need not collide, provide food or become visible to creature vision.

**Brain-controlled glands:** Separate inheritable organs could, on a brain output command, produce **additional pheromone objects** (possibly with a distinct heritable signature). These emissions **cost organism energy**. **Masking by mixture is explicitly chosen**: glands may change the total chemical blend detected by other organisms, but there is **no chemical neutralization** and no reduction of an existing component by adding nonnegative concentrations. The odor emitted by the creature body and the involuntary passive marks remains present in the total signal. Quantity, timing, gland phenotype, cooldown, multiple signatures and maintenance expenses remain unresolved; the brain is not supplied with built-in messages or goals.

**Energy model to decide:**
- **Information marker without a separately spendable energy store:** Charge the emitter for active secretion and account for that expense through the closed-world energy ledger in line with existing dissipation rules. The marker holds sensory information, not collectible energy.
- **Physical chemical stock:** Transfer paid material energy from organism to droplet, persist it explicitly and return it exactly once upon decay/removal. Never conjure or discard energy.
- Explicitly decide whether passive deposition carries an energy cost. Do not assume that generating arbitrarily many persistent physical chemicals is free.

### Preservation, performance and compatibility constraints

- The simulation Worker is authoritative for emissions, positions, field state, nose sampling, energy, and expiries on deterministic fixed ticks, including off-camera and at high playback speeds. At x0 none of those lifetimes advance; display-only inspection remains allowed.
- Preserve genetic inheritance, lifetime neural learning, physical locomotion/feeding, ecology, energy conservation, current tube behavior and exact historical saves. New genes/parts/glands/markers would need deliberate schema and Bank compatibility; legacy bodies cannot silently acquire a new sense.
- **IDEA-023 performance is still P0.** A naive all-emitters × all-noses query and per-droplet alpha compositing can be expensive. Any implementation proposal needs a measured performance budget and an efficient, deterministic local spatial sampling approach (for example bounded emissions/spatial indexing), keeping renderer refresh and Worker tick rate separate.
- Eventual validation would need tests for overlapping fields, mixed channel readings, source direction not being secretly injected, absence of nose, genetics, costs, droplet expiry, tube boundaries, old saves/PRNG continuation, off-camera Worker execution and observer visualization isolation. Ecology/performance comparisons require a separately authorized bounded protocol.
- This future chemical system is **not** an extension of the own-egg/offspring retinal proposal in upstream #118. No feature coding, tuning, trials, PR to upstream, or change to VIDA 3D is authorized by these notes.

## Questions still open

1. **Naming is still open:** the user prefers distinct mnemonic concepts matching organic/blood-like nutrients (R), inorganic/mineral nutrients (G), and animal bodies (B). Candidate **sanguine/mineral/musky** has been proposed, not accepted. **Encoding clarified:** RRGGBB is the immutable pulse signature (0–255 per channel); TT and scalar pulse intensity have a 1:1 normalized correspondence (00 no scent/transparent, FF maximum/opaque). Sensor input scaling, spatial falloff, and additive saturation still need design.
2. **Unified pulse and overlap rules established conceptually:** pulses expand and decay faster than area dilution, disappear from authoritative state at zero, and noses sum all overlapping floral/herbal/mineral contributions. Still choose cadence, source strength, radial falloff, decay-to-zero rule and the rarely reached per-channel saturation threshold.
2a. **Residual pulses:** emitted circles remain independently active after a source moves/disappears. Should they remain centered at release positions, drift with water, or have another explicitly modeled transport rule?
2b. **Resource bookkeeping:** how are finite perfume units, source depletion and active/passive secretion expense paid for without making infinite-source nutrients/organisms violate closed-world energy accounting?
3. **Direct creature emission restored:** a creature continuously/repeatedly emits body-scent circles somewhat stronger than its passive pheromone scent, and also deposits weak pheromone objects. Which locations, frequencies and intensities should apply to each? Should inherited genes separately govern body odor and passive marks?
4. How many noses, where, at what sensing cost, and with what optional per-channel sensitivity?
5. What passive pheromone deposition frequency, finite unit count, weak per-pulse intensity, pulse cadence, total entity cap, lifetime and energy cost are suitable? The one-per-second and three-units examples are not requirements.
6. **Masking by mixture is chosen, not neutralization.** Are active glands limited to one signature, or can there be multiple genetically distinct emitted signals? What range of gland intensity allows useful modulation without overwhelming receptors?
7. Do droplets contain energy-bearing chemical material or only a sensory marker? How do decay, water movement and tubes interact with them?
8. How will we maintain acceptable CPU, Worker-to-UI traffic and renderer cost given the existing performance audit?
